# Peer review

Review the manuscript against repository evidence, not its rhetoric. Separately assess significance, novelty, technical correctness, experimental design, baseline strength, statistical support, reproducibility, clarity, related-work coverage, limitations, and ethical/data concerns.

For each objection, cite the manuscript location and relevant repository evidence. Classify severity:

- **Blocking:** invalidates a central claim or submission readiness.
- **Major:** materially weakens confidence or contribution.
- **Minor:** improves clarity, completeness, or presentation.
- **Question:** requires an author explanation or evidence pointer.

Identify claims that exceed results, missing alternatives, inadequate comparisons, leakage risks, selective reporting, and citation gaps. Steelman the contribution before criticizing it. Do not issue fake conference scores, acceptance predictions, or invented reviewer consensus.

For every blocking or major objection, propose the cheapest valid experiment, analysis, citation search, or revision that could resolve it. State what outcome would resolve the concern and what outcome would require weakening the claim. Finish with a prioritized revision plan and residual risks.
